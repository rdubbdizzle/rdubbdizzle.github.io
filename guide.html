How Space-A actually works

Space-Available means you fly after duty passengers. The jet is real. The seat is a maybe until they call your name.

Who can fly

Active duty on leave, some dependents, retirees, and 100% P&T disabled veterans (among others) may compete. Selection is by category, then by signup date and time inside that category. Marking present does not jump you in line.

Always confirm eligibility in DoDI 4515.13 and the terminal’s own rules. This site is not the regulation.

Signup

Most terminals take email. Send travel documents, not PII like SSN. One active signup per terminal. You can usually stay on the list for a window of about 60 days or through your leave dates. Sign up as soon as you are eligible — that timestamp is your seniority.

Reading the 72-hour board

AMC terminals publish a rolling three-day outlook. Earlier than that, plans change constantly. Seat codes:

  • F — firm seats the crew expects to release
  • T — tentative; still show up
  • TBD — unpublished until close to departure; can become dozens of seats

Roll call time is local to the terminal. Be marked present before it. Many terminals will not take you present more than 24 hours out.

Roll call day

Arrive early. Have physical copies of leave, ID, passports (valid well past return), and dependent documents. Two checked bags, 70 pounds, 62 linear inches is the usual C-17 standard — some airframes are stricter. If they call your category, you’re flying. If they don’t, you were never going to be on that jet, and the next board is already the job.
